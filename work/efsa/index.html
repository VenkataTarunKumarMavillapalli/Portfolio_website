Data quality and internal tools at EFSA

I worked on data quality dashboards, CRM checks, Databricks applications and documentation for data products.

About the work

At EFSA, I worked in the Integrated Data Unit on data management and product support. Much of my work focused on checking that information was consistent across systems and making those checks easier for other teams to use.

I worked with analysts, engineers, product managers, domain specialists and scientists. Alongside implementation, I wrote documentation so that people could understand how the products worked and continue using them.

Checking data quality

I designed four Power BI dashboards with more than 30 automated business rules and Terraform-based Databricks pipelines. The work covered more than 100,000 CRM records, six dimensions of data quality and three business units: FDP, ENREL and KNOW. The dashboards made discrepancies visible and supported ongoing monitoring.

Reducing repetitive ticket updates

I built the Azure DevOps Closed Date Updater, a secure Databricks application connected to the Azure DevOps API. It automated updates for more than 25 Agile tickets each month, kept a complete audit trail and saved more than five hours of monthly manual work.

Comparing information across systems

I created a Salesforce–Databricks comparison pipeline that automated the export and validation of more than 59,000 CRM records. I also refactored five Python notebooks into modular Databricks components, improving the reliability and maintainability of the Nos extractor.

Making the work maintainable

I used Databricks branches and Azure DevOps commits to prepare changes for controlled deployment. I wrote user and developer documentation for three EFSA data products, covering architecture, setup, data logic and troubleshooting, and supported ad hoc CRM analyses.

Building with other trainees

I was a founding member of the trainee-powered scrum team under HUCAP. We worked on an internal platform for sharing ad hoc learning and collaboration opportunities across EFSA units. This brought product design and cross-team collaboration into my data-focused role.

What this work involved

The work made data checks more repeatable, reduced routine updates and gave colleagues clearer information about how the data products operated.

Tools and methods: Power BI, Databricks, Python, Terraform, Azure DevOps, Salesforce.
